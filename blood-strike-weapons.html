Choose a weapon for the fight you expect.

A weapon is useful when its strengths match the distance, pace and situations you regularly face. Damage numbers alone do not tell the whole story. Recoil, time needed to aim, reload downtime, magazine size and your ability to control the weapon all affect how it performs in a real match.

Updated November 2026 · By MOVVS33

Think in roles rather than rankings

Instead of asking which weapon is always the best, decide what role you need your weapon to fill. A close-range option should feel responsive when an opponent appears nearby. A more flexible option may be easier to use across changing distances. A longer-range weapon may reward patience and accurate positioning but can be less forgiving when an opponent closes the gap.

Five things to compare

  1. Effective range. Consider the distances at which you can reliably land shots. A weapon that feels strong nearby may become difficult to control farther away.
  2. Recoil pattern. Spend time firing controlled bursts and longer sprays in practice. Notice whether the weapon pulls vertically, shifts sideways or becomes difficult to correct.
  3. Handling and aim time. Fast movement and quick aiming can matter when you frequently enter buildings or reposition during a fight.
  4. Magazine and reload downtime. A large magazine can help in extended engagements, but reload time and ammunition use still matter.
  5. Attachment trade-offs. An attachment that reduces recoil may affect mobility or handling. Choose based on the weakness you are trying to solve.

Build a balanced loadout

Think about the gaps in your preferred weapon. If it is difficult to control at distance, consider whether an optic or recoil-focused attachment helps without making the weapon too slow. If your setup feels sluggish in close quarters, prioritise handling and movement where the available attachments allow it. Avoid adding attachments simply because they are unlocked; each should solve a specific problem.

Weapon balance and attachment effects can change with game updates. Check the current in-game descriptions and test a build after major patches instead of relying on an old tier list.

Test weapons fairly

Use the same practice conditions when comparing two weapons. Test similar distances, target movement and firing patterns. Start with short bursts, then practise longer sprays. Compare how quickly you can acquire a target, how much correction you need, and how often you have to reload. If possible, repeat the test over multiple sessions so one unusually good attempt does not decide the result.

Match the weapon to your control setup

A weapon that requires frequent recoil correction may feel very different depending on your sensitivity and button layout. If you struggle to keep a sight on target, first check whether your control setup is comfortable and consistent. Our Blood Strike controls guide explains a step-by-step sensitivity routine.

Keep a simple comparison note

After testing, write down the weapon, attachments, distance, what felt comfortable and what caused problems. For example: “easy to track at medium range, but the longer spray needs more correction.” This is more useful than writing only “good” or “bad,” because it tells you when to choose that weapon and what to practise next.

Final checklist

  • Does the weapon suit the distances you usually fight at?
  • Can you control its recoil without losing your target?
  • Do the attachments improve a weakness without creating a bigger one?
  • Does the weapon fit your movement and aiming habits?
  • Have you tested it after the latest game update?
