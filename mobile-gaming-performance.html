Diagnose lag before changing every setting.

A game that feels slow or inconsistent does not always have a graphics problem. Frame-rate drops, network latency, device heat, battery limits and background activity can feel similar during a match. Identify the symptom first, then change the setting most likely to affect it.

Updated November 2026 · By MOVVS33

Understand the difference between FPS and ping

Frame rate describes how frequently the device renders new images. A low or unstable frame rate can make camera movement look uneven and touch input feel less responsive. Ping is the time it takes for data to travel between your device and the game server. High or unstable ping can make opponents appear to move irregularly or actions register late even when the picture looks smooth.

These are different problems. Lowering graphics settings may help a device maintain frame rate, but it will not repair a weak Wi-Fi signal or a distant server. Likewise, changing networks will not solve thermal throttling caused by a device becoming too hot.

Check device temperature and sustained performance

Phones and tablets can reduce performance when internal temperatures rise. A game may run smoothly at the beginning of a session and become less consistent later. Heavy charging, direct sunlight, thick cases, high screen brightness and demanding graphics can add heat. Test in a cool, ventilated environment and compare performance over a similar session length.

If the device becomes unusually hot, take a break and let it cool. Do not cover vents or use unsafe cooling methods. Avoid making a performance judgement from a short test immediately after opening the game; sustained behaviour is what matters during longer play sessions.

Use a repeatable graphics test

  1. Record the current graphics quality and frame-rate option.
  2. Choose a repeatable practice area or game mode.
  3. Test one graphics change at a time, keeping other settings stable.
  4. Observe whether movement remains smooth after several minutes, not just at the start.
  5. Check temperature and battery behaviour alongside frame rate.
  6. Keep the setting that gives the most stable experience without excessive heat or distracting visual loss.

Check network stability separately

Use a reliable Wi-Fi or mobile-data connection and avoid large downloads or streaming on the same network during a test. If possible, compare more than one location or network. A speed test can show general throughput, but gaming also depends on latency, jitter, packet loss and the route to the game server. A fast download result does not guarantee a stable match.

Reduce unnecessary background load

Close apps that are actively downloading, recording or synchronising when you do not need them. Keep the operating system and game updated through official channels. Check the device's built-in game mode and battery settings, but understand what each option changes. Battery-saving modes may restrict performance; high-performance modes can increase heat and power use.

Do not assume the highest advertised FPS setting is always the best choice. Your display, game support, temperature and sustained hardware performance all determine what you actually experience.

Make a simple troubleshooting log

When a problem occurs, note the game mode, approximate session length, graphics settings, device temperature, connection type and whether the issue is visual stutter or delayed online actions. This helps separate a device-performance problem from a network problem. Change one factor at a time and repeat the same test.

When to stop troubleshooting

If the device overheats, shuts down, shows battery swelling or behaves abnormally, stop gaming and seek qualified device support. Do not keep pushing a device that is showing signs of a hardware or battery fault. For ordinary performance questions, consult the device manufacturer's support information and the game's official update notes.
